Kit 01

Humanoid Learning Kit

A 35 cm robot in five snap-together modules. Each module is a lesson on its own: program the head, then an arm, then make it walk or drive.

    Height~355 mm
    Torso105 × 64 × 122 mm
    Walking version11 servos
    Wheeled version9 servos + 2 TT motors
    JoinsDovetails, clips, latches
    Power2 × 18650, 6 V servo rail

    Head

    An SG90 servo turns the head left and right. The HC-SR04 ultrasonic sensor is the face: its two round transducers are the eyes, and they measure how far away things are.

    Torso

    The brain lives here: ESP32, PCA9685 servo driver, L298N motor driver, a 6 V regulator and the battery. Wires run inside, out of sight. A USB window lets students plug in without opening it.

    Arms

    A shoulder and an elbow servo per arm, both hidden in the upper arm. Hands swap in seconds: a punching fist on one side, a blocking guard on the other.

    Legs, two ways

    Walking legs have hip, knee and ankle servos and wide feet. Wheeled legs keep the same thigh but end in a TT gear motor and a 66 mm wheel. Swap one pair for the other in a minute.

    Kit 02

    Bionic Arm Kit

    A three-finger gripper worn on the forearm. Squeeze your own muscle and the robot hand closes. Students learn how prosthetic hands read the body.

      1Muscle

      EMG pads on the forearm pick up the tiny electrical signal when a muscle tightens.

      2ESP32

      Smooths the signal and decides: is the student squeezing?

      3Servo + spool

      Each SG90 winds a fishing-line tendon onto a spool.

      4Fingers curl

      The tendon pulls the finger closed. An elastic cord opens it again.

      5Touch

      FSR pads in the fingertips feel the grip, so it can hold a paper cup without crushing it.

      Fingers2 + thumb
      Servo box141 × 68 × 48 mm
      Actuators3 × SG90
      SensorsEMG + 3 × FSR 402
      TendonsBraided fishing line
      Hinges1.75 mm filament pins

      Lesson 1 · Signals

      Watch the raw muscle signal, then smooth it with a moving average and pick a threshold.

      Lesson 2 · Mechanics

      Route tendons, feel friction, and see how spool size changes speed and force.

      Lesson 3 · Feedback

      Close the loop: let the fingertip sensors stop the grip at just the right force.

      Inside the kits

      Real components, not toys

      Every kit uses the same parts engineers use. Pick one to spin it around in 3D.

      Built for classrooms

      Made so a group of 13-year-olds can build it

      ◧

      Snap-fit, no screws

      Dovetails, clips and latches hold everything. No screwdrivers, nothing to strip, nothing to lose.

      ⌗

      Block programming

      Students program the ESP32 with Consica's drag-and-drop block editor. No syntax errors on day one.

      ◫

      Modular by design

      Every module plugs into the same socket standard, so students can design and print their own parts.

      ⌁

      Hidden wiring

      Wires run through hollow necks and channels inside the parts. The robot looks finished, not messy.

      ◎

      Made for groups

      One kit per group. Each student can own a module, then the team brings them together.

      ⎔

      Printable anywhere

      All structural parts are 3D-printed, so a broken piece is a reprint, not a reorder.
